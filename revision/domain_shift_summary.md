The web example is reversed: web's own R1 AUROC is 0.927 [0.898, 0.953], and R3 web is -0.065 [-0.095, -0.033] below it. LR-B shows no detected AUROC change in any fold (R3 − R1dm between -0.012 and +0.022). At 10% prevalence, LR-C's AUPRC falls in medical, banking, web, email and coding. The GRU loses AUROC in every fold (-0.295 to -0.179), and so does GRU-drop0.2, by less (-0.203 to -0.076). In-distribution, the LR-C over LR-B advantage is detected only in web, email and coding. In R3, LR-C ranks below LR-B in medical, banking, web, email and coding (-0.063 to -0.026). The AUROC reversal is therefore established in web, email and coding only; medical and banking have no detected in-distribution advantage to reverse. For the GRU against LR-C, the in-distribution advantage is detected in medical, banking, email and coding (web -0.002 [-0.030, +0.023]), and the R3 deficit in medical, banking, web, email and coding. The reversal is established in medical, banking, email and coding. Tool-block dropout shrinks the LR-C deficit against LR-B to between -0.015 and -0.001. Small as they are, those deficits are still detected in medical, web, email and coding, so under the rule the reversal survives the remedy in web, email and coding, at a much smaller size. GRU-drop0.2 shows no detected R3 deficit against LR-C in any fold after Holm (-0.003 to +0.026), so the GRU reversal does not survive dropout. On AUPRC@10%, the C vs B reversal is established in banking, web and email, and the GRU vs C reversal in medical. TFIDF beats LR-C in every domain in R1 (+0.071 to +0.213) and in R3 (+0.118 to +0.251). Its own R3 loss is small (-0.018 to -0.004) but detected in medical, banking, web, email and coding. These numbers do not explain the reversal (M1 covers the mechanism). They also do not show that medical and banking lack an in-distribution advantage: the domain-matched R1 pools hold only 276 to 345 trajectories, and "not detected" there means the intervals are wide, not that the advantage is zero. The R1dm and R3 pools also share trajectories (see m3), which makes the independent-bootstrap intervals conservative.

## Deviations and analysis choices (also appended to revision/DEVIATIONS.md, section 'Domain shift analysis')

- DS1. GRU seeds. The plan does not say how the three GRU training seeds enter intervals. Following evalkit.bootstrap (the v0.3 convention used by the GRU cache summary), each bootstrap replicate uses one randomly drawn seed and the point estimate is the mean over seeds. Where the seeds disagree strongly (R3 banking and coding thresholds), this gives very wide intervals, so per-seed rows (GRU#seedk) are also reported for the threshold analysis.
- DS2. Holm p values. The plan asks for Holm correction but does not say how to get p values from a bootstrap. We use the two-sided percentile-bootstrap p = min(1, 2*min(1+#{d<=0}, 1+#{d>=0})/(B+1)). 'Detected' (unadjusted) keeps the plan 3.3 definition (95% percentile interval excludes 0). The two criteria can disagree only at the boundary.
- DS3. Reversal metric. Plan 3.4 does not name the metric. AUROC is primary because the manuscript's reversal claim is about AUROC; AUPRC@10% is reported as secondary. The rule is also applied to the dropout pairs (LR-C-drop vs LR-B, GRU-drop0.2 vs LR-C), together with the plan 5 question of whether the original reversal survives the remedy.
- DS4. m5 multiplicity. For the claim of no loss, detection without correction is the primary criterion because it is the stricter test for that claim; Holm over split seeds (pooled) and over domains (domain-matched) is reported as secondary. A macro (mean of per-domain) AUROC is added to remove the domain-mix change.
- DS5. m2. The task-cluster bootstrap resamples the 35 R2 test tasks with replacement (not stratified by class; every task contains both classes). The R1 world-cluster sensitivity resamples the 676 pool-E worlds. AUPRC@10% weights are recomputed per replicate from its own class counts.
- DS6. m12. Classifier two-sample test: StandardScaler + LogisticRegression(class_weight='balanced', max_iter=5000) on representation B, StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle, seed 0) on train-versus-test membership. The interval is a stratified bootstrap of the out-of-fold scores (it omits refitting variability). The primary variant uses all rows of the setting's train and test sets; a pool-E-rows variant (failed attacks excluded on both sides) is a sensitivity analysis. The test rows equal the cache rows in every setting (asserted).
- DS7. Additions not in the plan: world-cluster sensitivity for the M3 comparisons (plan 3.5 names world clustering as a sensitivity for R1/R3), pattern-standardised AUROC and per-pattern AUROC/recall (m13), a joint-overlap bootstrap (m3), and derived precision at 2%/10% prevalence from recall and FPR (M4 values).
- DS8. The plan names B = 2,000 for the AUPRC bootstrap; B = 2,000 is used for every metric here. Intervals therefore differ slightly from the cache summaries (B = 1,000 for AgentDrift in validation.json).
- DS9. m5 overlap (addition). The R1 and R1-wd pool-E test sets share 287-327 trajectories per split seed. The plan asks for independent bootstraps, which are reported as primary. An overlap-preserving bootstrap (resample the union of both pools, stratified by class, evaluate each pool on its own resampled rows) is added as a sensitivity analysis, together with 10 RNG reruns of the borderline LR-B seed-1 comparison. The overlap-aware version detects that loss before correction.
- DS10. Monte Carlo stability (addition). Every Holm family of M3, M4 and m5 (and the m2 comparisons) with a member whose p or pH lies in [0.03, 0.07] at B = 2,000 is recomputed at B = 10,000 with fresh RNG streams. The pre-specified B = 2,000 values stay primary; decisions that change are listed.
- DS11. m12 extra (addition). The C2ST is also reported for R1wd_s1-s4, LOSO_* and, per cross-validation pair, WUT, WUT_F and WIT, and over C2ST fold-assignment seeds 0-4. The task asks only for R1, R1wd_s0, R2, R3 and R4.
