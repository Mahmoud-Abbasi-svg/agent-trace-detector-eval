### Plain reading

The manuscript's world count and purity do not reproduce. Under the key that the world-disjoint split itself uses, the 10,656 pooled trajectories contain 1,133 worlds, with purity 0.883 and largest worlds 120/95/91. The same key gives 1,141 worlds on the full corpus, exactly as the AgentDrift README states. Four of 1,716 key, population and label combinations give 1,029 worlds. None of them also gives the published largest sizes or the 54 worlds with 2,724 trajectories, and in the pool alone the candidate keys give 67 different world counts, so a hit on 1,029 is weak evidence. We therefore cannot say which key produced 1,029, and the manuscript should report 1,133 worlds and purity 0.883 [0.860, 0.909]. The figures 194/169/144 in PRE_ABLATION_DESIGN.md reproduce under no key tried. The other v0.3 diagnostics do reproduce: 99.3% world reuse, AUROC 0.963 (base rate 0.441), the majority baseline 0.558, and 54 worlds with 2,724 trajectories. World reuse is a real leakage path in R1 and in R2 outside email. There, worlds are almost label-pure (purity 0.957 to 0.995), and the training-world majority label predicts the test label with 0.94 to 0.99 accuracy. Email worlds are large and mixed, with a lookup accuracy of 0.48. These numbers show that the split *allows* a world shortcut. They do not show that any detector *uses* it. Only the world-disjoint control, reported by the domain-shift stream, can test that. Prevalence differs by domain because of the design: in pool E, banking is 0.401 and every other domain is 0.522. Banking's positives also differ in hijack mix (34.7% full hijacks against 58.5% to 60.6% elsewhere), so cross-fold comparisons that involve banking confound prevalence and pattern mix.

## m15. Domain and world probes

**Question.** Can representation A (bag of tools), B (the 10 behavioural features) or B-minus (B without the two keyword state-changing features) identify the domain, or the world? Does any of them beat the floors (the majority class; for the world probe, a classifier that sees only the domain)?

**Design.** StandardScaler + LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', solver='lbfgs', multinomial, max_iter=5000). C is chosen by an inner grid search over 9 values from 1e-05 to 1000, scored on accuracy, with inner folds StratifiedKFold(3, shuffle=True, random_state=100+10*rep+fold), GridSearchCV scoring='accuracy'. The outer folds are StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state in [0, 1, 2]), so every trajectory gets 3 out-of-fold predictions. Accuracy is the mean over the 3 repetitions, and the 95% interval is a trajectory-level bootstrap. Differences are paired: both units are evaluated on the same resampled rows. The A vocabulary is tools occurring in the outer training fold. The world probe uses worlds with at least 30 trajectories in the pool (`meta.probe_design`). ### Plain reading

*What the probes support.* (1) Domain identity is fully encoded in tool identity: A and C reach accuracy 1.000 against a majority floor of 0.207. It is partly encoded in the behavioural features: B reaches 0.458 [0.449, 0.467]. This does not come only from the keyword features, since B-minus still reaches 0.381, and it is not a label effect, since B reaches 0.505 on benign trajectories alone against a floor of 0.200. A domain shortcut is therefore available to every representation, and fully available to A and C. (2) None of the linear probes identifies worlds better than a classifier that knows only the domain. A equals the domain-only floor (difference -0.0005 [-0.005, +0.004]), while B and B-minus fall *below* it (detected). B-minus does not detectably beat even the majority floor. On the four label-pure domains (supplementary probe, 301 worlds) A is also detectably below domain-only.

*What the probes do not support.* They cannot show that world identity is 'not encoded'. A linear model with about 50 trajectories per class that fails to decode 54 classes is a null result about that probe, not evidence that the information is absent. Non-linear detectors, such as the GRU or a TF-IDF model that reads names and addresses, may still recover it. The main probe population is also 81% email, where worlds carry almost no label information (purity 0.573 there), so the probe measures the part of world identity that matters least for leakage. The manuscript's sentence 'our features do not encode world identity' should become 'linear probes on A, B and B-minus do not identify worlds beyond what the domain alone gives'. Conclusions about world-level leakage should rest on the world-disjoint control, as the review asks. The model-identity probe on AgentDojo, the other half of m15, is outside this task and is not re-run here.
